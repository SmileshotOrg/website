### **2. About us**

As part of our service, we provide the SmileShot Platform (Platform) to qualified dental professionals **(including practice owners, self-employed associates, and clinical team members acting in a professional capacity)** (Practitioners). The Platform facilitates the secure administration and file-management of clinical dental photographs **using a "Bring Your Own Storage" (BYOS) architecture.**

To maintain strict compliance, SmileShot adheres to rigid operational boundaries: it is not a clinical diagnostic tool, it is not a primary clinical record system, and it is not an image hosting platform or a backup drive. We act purely as a Data Processor, while you act as the Data Controller.

**SmileShot operates strictly at the directory metadata level. The Platform infrastructure lacks the technical capability, access permissions, and functionality to view, open, inspect, render, or visually process the underlying clinical photographs stored in your cloud environment.**

The Platform provides clinical administrative support by securely streamlining practice workflows and visual record management. It utilises automated tools to extract essential daily scheduling information for patient profile creation and pairs this with an intelligent visual interface that securely organises a practitioner’s existing clinical photographs. By linking these clinical images to their corresponding patient profiles, the Platform creates a highly searchable and structured visual database designed to facilitate case management and treatment presentations.

### **3.1 Cloud Storage Boundaries & Sandboxed Architecture (BYOS)**

**SmileShot operates under rigid technical isolation to safeguard your practice and patient data:**

- **Sandboxed Access to One Folder Only:** When connecting your third-party cloud storage account (e.g., Google Drive, Microsoft OneDrive, Dropbox), you grant technical access strictly and exclusively to a single root directory labelled **"SmileShot"**. SmileShot does not request, hold, or execute permissions to access, view, index, modify, or interact with any other folders, directories, or practice files outside this designated folder.

- **Technical Inability to View or Ingest Images:** Our infrastructure operates purely at the metadata and directory level. SmileShot **lacks the technical capability, access permissions, and functionality to view, open, inspect, render, or visually process** the underlying visual image payloads stored in your cloud storage.

- **No Central Image Hosting:** All raw clinical photography remains stored exclusively within your designated third-party cloud environment. We do not copy, backup, or host your raw image files on SmileShot servers.

#### **7.1 Daylist Processing & Ephemeral AI Infrastructure**

When using Daylist Capture to automate patient profile creation, appointment sheet imagery is processed transiently using enterprise cloud infrastructure provided by Microsoft Azure OpenAI located within the European Economic Area (specifically Sweden).

Under UK GDPR adequacy regulations, data transfers between the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area (EEA) are formally recognised by the UK Government as providing an equivalent, high standard of data protection.

This operational processing adheres to strict technical guardrails:

- Ephemeral Processing (Zero Disk Storage): The appointment image is processed transiently in temporary computer memory (RAM) solely for the few seconds required to extract three administrative fields (patient name, date of birth, and appointment time). The source image is irretrievably and permanently discarded from temporary memory immediately upon extraction.

- No AI Model Training: Microsoft Azure enterprise terms and SmileShot architecture ensure that daylist images, extracted text, and patient identifiers are never logged, retained, or used to train, retrain, or improve any artificial intelligence or machine learning models.

- In-Transit Encryption: All data transferred to and from the Azure OpenAI processing endpoint is encrypted using TLS 1.3 encryption.
